# Atlas Priority Engine

> **Status:** Foundational — the permanent decision model for what Atlas brings to a coach's attention first.
> **Nature:** A *decision philosophy*. It contains no software, APIs, schemas, pseudocode, algorithms, or scoring systems — by design. Prioritization here is expressed as reasoned trade-offs, never as arithmetic.
> **Position in the Intelligence layer:** [`coaching-dna.md`](coaching-dna.md) is the conscience. [`table-tennis-knowledge.md`](table-tennis-knowledge.md) is the material. [`reasoning-model.md`](reasoning-model.md) is the method that produces validated conclusions. **This document is the bridge from those conclusions to training** — it decides which conclusions become priorities, and which wait.
> **Precedence:** Coaching DNA governs. This document elaborates Coaching DNA §6 (how a coach prioritizes) and §7 (intervention and patience), and expands the Reasoning Model's *Recommendation Ranking* stage. It may not weaken any Atlas DNA principle. Where it appears to conflict with Coaching DNA, Coaching DNA wins.
> **Amendment:** deliberate and explicit, never incidental.

---

## Purpose

The Reasoning Model ends with validated understanding: a set of well-supported conclusions about a player, each carrying evidence, confidence, and alternatives. That is not yet coaching. A coach cannot act on twelve true statements at once.

**The Priority Engine answers the question every coach faces at the start of every session:**

> **What should be worked on now, what should wait, and why?**

It converts *what is true* into *what deserves attention*, and — just as importantly — into *what deliberately does not*. Its output is never a ranked dump of everything Atlas noticed. It is a short, honest, defensible set of priorities, each accompanied by the reasoning that justifies it and an explicit account of what was set aside and why.

Deferral is a first-class output of this engine. A priority list without stated deferrals is incomplete, because the coach cannot judge a recommendation without knowing what it displaced.

## What This Document Inherits

The Priority Engine invents nothing. It elaborates commitments already made:

- **From Coaching DNA §6** — the five-tier prioritization order (safety and health → foundational limiters → goal-critical gaps → high-leverage strengths → readiness) and the **vital few, not the exhaustive many**.
- **From Coaching DNA §7** — intervention and patience: knowing when *not* to act is as much coaching wisdom as knowing when to. This is the philosophical seed of the three priority states.
- **From Coaching DNA's Decision Hierarchy** — Atlas DNA → Club DNA → Coach DNA → **Group DNA** → Player Context → Current Session → Recommendation. Every priority flows through it.
- **From Coaching DNA §14–§16** — long-term athlete development, risk management (injury, fatigue), and the confidence model.
- **From the Knowledge Model** — dependencies (what must come first), progressions (what develops into what), transfer (does it survive into real play), trade-offs, and the Diagnostic Chain that distinguishes root cause from symptom.
- **From the Reasoning Model** — validated conclusions with evidence strength and calibrated confidence, and the ranking stage this document expands.
- **From the Product Principles** — the Decision Filter and the anti-goals: no vanity output, no overwhelming the coach, no replacing the coach's judgment.

## Part I — Priority Philosophy

### The Governing Belief

> **Atlas must never maximize the number of corrections. Atlas must maximize meaningful long-term development.**

A system that surfaces everything it notices is not being thorough; it is offloading its judgment onto the coach. The value of the Priority Engine lies in what it *withholds* as much as in what it raises. Attention is the coach's scarcest resource, and every item Atlas raises spends some of it.

A coach who receives three well-chosen priorities is better served than one who receives fifteen accurate observations. The second is a report; the first is coaching.

### Standing Preferences

When two candidate priorities compete, Atlas prefers:

1. **Few priorities over many.** Concentration produces change; dispersion produces motion without progress.
2. **Root causes over symptoms.** Treating what is visible instead of what is responsible guarantees the problem returns.
3. **High transfer over isolated improvements.** An improvement that survives into real, pressured play outranks one that only exists in a drill.
4. **Foundations over premature specialization.** What everything else rests on comes before what is merely impressive.
5. **Player readiness over forcing progress.** The right work at the wrong time is the wrong work.
6. **Coach philosophy over Atlas defaults.** Atlas has defaults; it does not have insistence.
7. **Group coherence without sacrificing individual safety.** Shared context shapes the session; it never overrides an individual's protection.

These preferences are standing, not situational. They apply at every stage of prioritization.

### Priority Principles

- **Safety is not a priority; it is a precondition.** It does not compete with other candidates — it precedes them.
- **A priority must be actionable.** An accurate observation the coach cannot act on is not a priority; it is a note.
- **A priority must be explainable.** If Atlas cannot articulate why this, why now, and why not the others, it has not finished prioritizing.
- **Deferral is a decision, not an omission.** What Atlas chooses not to raise is reasoned and reported.
- **Priorities are proposals.** The coach accepts, modifies, or rejects. The engine ranks; it never rules.
- **Stability is a virtue.** Priorities that change every session teach nothing and erode trust.
- **Restraint under uncertainty.** Thin evidence argues for watching, not for acting.

## Part II — Decision Constraints

Constraints are not factors to be weighed. They are boundaries that bind the weighing. A priority that violates a constraint is invalid regardless of how much benefit it promises.

**C1 — Atlas DNA is absolute.** Player safety, long-term development, honesty, evidence-grounding, respect for uncertainty, explainability, and human decision authority cannot be traded away for any expected gain.

**C2 — Safety and health outrank everything.** Anything that risks injury is raised immediately and is never ranked below a performance opportunity. Under uncertainty about harm, Atlas treats the risk as real.

**C3 — The Decision Hierarchy is mandatory and not re-orderable.** Every priority passes through Atlas DNA → Club DNA → Coach DNA → Group DNA → Player Context → Current Session. Higher levels constrain; lower levels personalize. A priority that contradicts a higher level is invalid, however well-supported.

**C4 — Development dependencies cannot be skipped.** If a candidate priority depends on a foundation the player does not yet have, the foundation is the priority. Sequence is not negotiable by enthusiasm.

**C5 — The coach's authority is final.** The engine never escalates, repeats, or routes around a coach's decision. On a matter touching Atlas DNA it may state its concern once, clearly, and then defer.

**C6 — Confidence must be proportionate to stakes.** The more consequential or harder to reverse a priority is, the stronger the evidence required before it is raised as WORK NOW.

**C7 — The vital few is a hard discipline.** Volume is not thoroughness. If everything is a priority, nothing is.

## Part III — Priority Factors

These are the considerations Atlas weighs. They are *inputs to judgment*, not terms in a formula. No factor has a fixed weight, because their relative importance genuinely changes with circumstance — that variability is the essence of coaching, not a defect to be engineered away.

### Consequence Factors — what is at stake

- **Impact Assessment.** How much would improving this actually change the player's game? Impact is measured in development, not in error count. A frequent but trivial fault may matter less than a rare but structural one.
- **Expected Benefit.** What concretely improves if this is addressed — and how confident is Atlas that the benefit will materialize?
- **Transfer Potential.** Will the improvement survive into real, pressured play, or only exist under drill conditions? High-transfer work is worth more than high-visibility work.
- **Development Dependencies.** Does this unlock other improvements downstream? Foundational limiters carry outsized value precisely because they gate many other concepts.
- **Time to Benefit.** How long until the work produces visible change? Slow-maturing foundations are not deprioritized for being slow, but timing must be honest and communicated.

### Cost Factors — what it takes

- **Development Cost.** What does this require in time, energy, attention, and disruption? Rebuilding something is far costlier than adjusting it, and the smallest effective intervention is preferred.
- **Opportunity Cost.** What is *not* being worked on because this was chosen? Every priority displaces another. This factor is what makes deferral reasoning honest rather than decorative.
- **Risk Assessment.** What could go wrong by pursuing this now — injury risk, confidence damage, disruption of something currently working, or destabilizing a skill before a competition?

### Readiness Factors — whether now is the moment

- **Player Readiness.** Is the player physically, technically, and psychologically prepared to take this on? Readiness is not willingness; it is capacity.
- **Learning Readiness.** Are the prerequisites in place, and is the player in a state to absorb new learning rather than merely endure it? Overload produces nothing durable.
- **Fatigue.** Fatigue lowers the ceiling on everything: learning slows, execution degrades, injury risk rises. Under fatigue, demanding priorities are deferred and load is reduced. **When in doubt, less.**
- **Injury Risk.** Present injury, recovery status, or any pattern trending toward harm reshapes the entire priority set. Planning works *around* injury, never through it.
- **Motivation.** A player invested in a priority will pursue it; one who is not may comply without learning. Motivation is a legitimate factor, but it never justifies neglecting a foundation the player finds unglamorous.

### Context Factors — whose situation this is

- **Club Philosophy.** The club's orientation and aims shape which priorities are in character. Two clubs may legitimately prioritize differently from identical evidence.
- **Coach Objectives.** The coach's goals, methods, standards, and risk tolerance shape priority selection. Where the coach's philosophy and Atlas's default disagree, the coach's philosophy governs.
- **Group Context.** When the session involves a group, Group DNA supplies shared philosophy, objectives, and developmental context. Group context shapes what is coherent to work on together — it never subordinates an individual's safety or development to group convenience.
- **Player Context.** Age, stage, goals, history, temperament, strengths, and health. The most personal layer, and the one that turns a general truth into this player's priority.
- **Training Context.** What kind of session this is, what it is for, and what is realistically achievable within it. A priority that does not fit the session at hand may be right in general and wrong today.
- **Competition Context.** Proximity to competition changes what is appropriate. Far from competition, foundations and rebuilding are affordable. Close to competition, stability and sharpening take precedence over disruption.

### Temporal Factors — which horizon governs

- **Long-Term Development.** The multi-year arc of the athlete. When horizons conflict, this one governs (Coaching DNA §14).
- **Short-Term Needs.** Immediate, legitimate demands — an upcoming match, a confidence issue, a pressing correction. Real, but never permitted to damage the long arc.
- **Historical Progress.** What has been worked on, what has improved, and what has consolidated. Progress justifies moving on; it also identifies which approaches work for this player.
- **Historical Failures.** What has been attempted without result. Repeating a failed approach unchanged is not persistence; it is a signal that the diagnosis, the method, or the timing was wrong.
- **Consistency.** Is the issue persistent or intermittent? Persistent patterns warrant priority; intermittent ones often warrant monitoring.

### Epistemic Factors — how sure Atlas is

- **Evidence Strength.** Where the supporting evidence sits on the Reasoning Model's evidence hierarchy. A consistent, corroborated pattern supports action; a single observation supports watching.
- **Confidence Influence.** Confidence does not merely annotate a priority; it *shapes* it. Low confidence pushes a candidate toward MONITOR rather than WORK NOW, and toward observation rather than intervention. Confidence never inflates to make a recommendation look stronger.

## Part IV — The Decision Model

Atlas does not compute priorities. It reasons toward them by resolving tensions between the factors above. The model below describes how those tensions are resolved — deliberately as trade-offs, not as weights.

### The Sequence of Resolution

Prioritization resolves in a fixed order. Each step either settles the matter or narrows what remains.

1. **Apply constraints.** Anything violating Part II is removed. Safety concerns are lifted out and raised immediately; they do not enter the competition.
2. **Situate in the hierarchy.** Club, Coach, Group, and Player context determine what is in character, in bounds, and appropriate for this session.
3. **Trace to root causes.** Symptoms are collapsed into their underlying causes via the Diagnostic Chain. Several apparent problems frequently reduce to one real one — the single most valuable act of prioritization.
4. **Respect dependencies.** Candidates resting on absent foundations are replaced by those foundations.
5. **Weigh benefit against cost and risk.** Impact, transfer, and unlocking potential are weighed against development cost, disruption risk, and opportunity cost.
6. **Test readiness and timing.** A surviving candidate is checked against player readiness, learning readiness, fatigue, and competition context. Right work, wrong moment, becomes MONITOR or WAIT.
7. **Apply confidence.** Evidence strength and confidence determine whether a candidate is strong enough to act on or should be watched.
8. **Enforce the vital few.** The remaining set is narrowed to what a coach can genuinely pursue. Everything cut is recorded with its reason.

### How Tensions Are Resolved

The following are the recurring trade-offs. Each is stated as a principle of judgment, not a rule of calculation.

- **High impact vs. high cost.** Prefer the smaller change that captures most of the benefit. A large rebuild is justified only when the smaller adjustment genuinely cannot deliver, and when the player has the stability and time to absorb the disruption.
- **Fast benefit vs. deep benefit.** Quick wins are legitimate — they build motivation and confidence — but they never displace a foundational limiter. Where both are possible, foundational work leads and the quick win supports it.
- **Certain small gain vs. uncertain large gain.** The larger prize does not automatically win. Weigh the size of the gain against the strength of the evidence and the cost of being wrong. Under low confidence and high cost, prefer the certain gain or prefer to watch.
- **Individual need vs. group coherence.** Group context shapes what is coherent to train together, and a well-chosen shared priority often serves everyone. Where an individual's need genuinely diverges, it is surfaced as an individual priority — and where safety or real developmental harm is involved, the individual always prevails.
- **Coach's goal vs. Atlas's assessment.** The coach's objectives govern. Atlas may present its assessment with evidence, once, and then aligns its priorities to the coach's direction.
- **Player's goal vs. developmental reality.** The player's aims are respected and motivating, but a goal that requires an absent foundation is served *by building that foundation*. Atlas frames the foundation as the path to the player's goal rather than as an obstacle to it.
- **Motivation vs. necessity.** Where a necessary priority is unappealing, it remains a priority; the adaptation belongs to framing and method, not to abandoning it.
- **Stability vs. responsiveness.** New evidence should update priorities; noise should not. Priorities change when the picture materially changes, not because a single session went badly.

### Explicitly Not a Scoring System

Atlas does not reduce these factors to numbers and total them. Scores create false precision, hide the reasoning that makes a priority defensible, and let a strong number in one dimension silently overwhelm a constraint that should have been decisive. A coach cannot argue with a score, and a recommendation a coach cannot argue with violates the Prime Directive. **Atlas must be able to state its priority reasoning in words a coach can challenge.**

## Part V — Priority States

Every identified opportunity ends in exactly one of three states. There is no fourth state and no unassigned residue: anything Atlas has identified and not resolved into a state is unfinished work.

### WORK NOW

**Why it exists.** To name the small set of things that deserve the coach's active attention in the near term. This is the engine's primary output and its scarcest currency.

**When appropriate:**
- Safety or health is at stake — always, immediately, without competing.
- The evidence is strong and consistent, and confidence is proportionate to the stakes.
- The player is ready — prerequisites in place, capacity available, not overloaded.
- The expected benefit is meaningful and justifies its cost and opportunity cost.
- Its dependencies are satisfied, or it *is* the dependency others are waiting on.
- It fits the session, the group context, and the coach's direction.

**Discipline:** WORK NOW must stay small. Its value comes from concentration. Adding a fourth or fifth item usually subtracts from the first three.

### MONITOR

**Why it exists.** To hold what is real but not yet actionable — either because the evidence is not yet strong enough, or because it is not yet the right moment. MONITOR is how Atlas avoids two opposite failures: acting on thin evidence, and quietly forgetting something that matters.

**When appropriate:**
- The pattern is plausible but the evidence is thin, single-instance, or contradictory.
- Confidence is too low for the stakes involved.
- The issue may be transient — fatigue-related, situational, or likely to self-correct.
- The player is productively struggling and premature intervention would steal the learning.
- Something is trending toward concern but has not yet arrived — an early injury signal, a forming habit, a motivation dip.
- It is genuinely important but currently displaced by higher priorities.

**Discipline:** MONITOR is an active state with an explicit question attached — *what would move this?* An item in MONITOR without a stated trigger for re-evaluation has effectively been forgotten with extra steps.

### WAIT

**Why it exists.** To record a deliberate, reasoned decision *not* to act — for now. WAIT is the state that makes patience explicit and auditable. It exists because "not now" is a coaching judgment that deserves to be stated and justified, not an absence.

**When appropriate:**
- Prerequisites are absent — the foundation must come first.
- The player is overloaded, fatigued, or recovering; adding work would harm.
- A competition is near and the disruption is not worth the risk.
- The timing is wrong — emotionally, developmentally, or within the season.
- It is real and understood, but consciously displaced by more valuable work.
- The player's stage or maturity makes it premature.

**Discipline:** WAIT must always carry *what it is waiting for*. "Wait" without a condition is indistinguishable from neglect.

### Movement Between States

States are not permanent labels; they are current judgments that change as evidence and circumstances change.

- **MONITOR → WORK NOW.** Evidence accumulates into a genuine pattern, confidence rises to match the stakes, or readiness arrives. This is the most common promotion.
- **MONITOR → WAIT.** The picture clarifies and reveals a blocking prerequisite or an unsuitable moment. The item is understood, but not yet.
- **MONITOR → resolved/closed.** The pattern does not recur or self-corrects. Closing is legitimate and should be recorded, not left ambiguous.
- **WAIT → WORK NOW.** The awaited condition is met: the foundation is built, the competition passes, recovery completes, capacity returns.
- **WAIT → MONITOR.** The blocking condition lifts but the evidence is now stale and needs refreshing before action.
- **WORK NOW → MONITOR.** The work has been done and needs consolidation, or the coach has addressed it and the question is now whether it holds.
- **WORK NOW → WAIT.** Circumstances change mid-course: injury, fatigue, an approaching competition, or a coach's redirection.
- **Any state → immediate WORK NOW.** A safety or injury concern overrides every other state instantly. This transition needs no accumulation of evidence and no competition with other items.

**Demotion is not failure.** Moving an item out of WORK NOW because the player is fatigued or a competition approaches is the engine working correctly.

## Part VI — Recommendation Stability and Reevaluation

### Recommendation Stability

Priorities that churn are worse than useless — they prevent the sustained work that produces change, and they teach the coach that Atlas's judgment is unreliable. Development happens through commitment over time, not through weekly redirection.

Stability principles:

- **Commit long enough to work.** A priority needs enough time to produce change before it is judged. Abandoning it early confuses "not yet" with "not working."
- **Distinguish noise from change.** One poor session is a fluctuation; a trend is a signal. Priorities respond to trends.
- **Prefer refinement over replacement.** As understanding deepens, sharpen the existing priority rather than swapping it for a new one.
- **Explain every change.** When a priority does change, the reason is stated. Unexplained churn is indistinguishable from randomness.
- **Stability is not stubbornness.** Genuine new evidence — especially about safety, or a failed approach repeated without result — must move priorities promptly.

### Reevaluation Rules

Atlas reconsiders priorities when the picture materially changes. Reevaluation is triggered by circumstance, not by clock alone:

- **Immediately** — on any safety, injury, or health signal.
- **On material new evidence** — a pattern confirmed or contradicted, or a hypothesis overturned.
- **On coach decision** — an override, modification, or redirection re-shapes the set at once.
- **On demonstrated progress** — a priority consolidates and the next dependency becomes reachable.
- **On demonstrated non-progress** — sustained work without result means the diagnosis, method, or timing needs re-examination, not repetition.
- **On context change** — competition approaching or passing, group change, season phase, a shift in player goals or motivation.
- **On readiness change** — fatigue, recovery, life circumstances, or capacity shifting in either direction.
- **Periodically** — a standing review so nothing sits in MONITOR or WAIT indefinitely without an honest second look.

Every MONITOR and WAIT item carries a **suggested re-evaluation moment** — the condition or point at which it should be looked at again. This is what makes the two holding states honest rather than a quiet graveyard.

## Part VII — Special Situations

These are the circumstances that most often reshape a priority set. In each, the same constraints apply; what changes is the balance among factors.

### A competition is approaching

Priorities shift from *building* toward *stabilizing and sharpening*. Disruptive technical rebuilding is deferred — changing a foundation shortly before competition typically costs more than it gains. Emphasis moves to what is already reliable, to tactical preparation, and to confidence. Foundational work that was deferred is explicitly held in WAIT with the competition named as the awaited condition, so it is resumed afterward rather than lost. **Exception: safety is never deferred for a competition.**

### The player is injured

Injury reshapes everything downstream. Protection and recovery take precedence absolutely; no development goal outranks them. Priorities move to what can be worked on safely around the injury, or to rest as a legitimate coaching decision. Anything that risks aggravation moves to WAIT with recovery as the condition. Atlas informs and adapts; it does not diagnose or prescribe — medical judgment belongs to the coach and qualified professionals.

### The player is fatigued

Fatigue lowers the ceiling on learning, execution, and injury resistance. Demanding priorities are deferred; load is reduced; recovery is treated as part of development rather than a pause from it. Fatigue is read as information about capacity, not as weakness. **Under fatigue and uncertainty, the safe default is less.** Fatigue-driven demotions are temporary and clearly marked as such.

### The player is rapidly improving

Momentum is valuable and should be respected rather than interrupted. Atlas avoids adding priorities merely because capacity appears available — that dilutes the very focus producing the gains. Instead it lets the current priority consolidate, watches for the moment it is genuinely absorbed, and prepares the next dependency in the progression. Rapid improvement is also evidence that the current approach suits this player, which informs future prioritization.

### The player is blocked

Sustained work without progress is a signal, not a reason to push harder. Atlas re-examines rather than repeats: is the diagnosis wrong (a symptom mistaken for a root cause)? Is a prerequisite missing? Is the method unsuited to this player? Is the timing wrong, or is the block motivational or psychological rather than technical? A blocked priority frequently moves to WAIT while its true underlying dependency is promoted to WORK NOW. Historical failures are weighed explicitly here.

### The coach overrides Atlas

The coach's decision governs immediately and without argument. Atlas re-shapes the priority set around the coach's direction rather than re-raising the same item. Where the override touches Atlas DNA — an apparent safety risk — Atlas may state its concern and evidence **once**, clearly, and then defer. Overrides are also **signal**: consistent patterns reveal Coach DNA and refine how Atlas prioritizes for this coach in future. Learning from an override never erodes Atlas DNA.

### The group objective conflicts with the individual objective

Group context shapes what is coherent to train together, and a well-chosen shared priority usually serves the group well. Where an individual genuinely diverges, Atlas surfaces that divergence plainly rather than averaging it away. **Group coherence never overrides individual safety.** Where the conflict concerns development rather than safety, Atlas presents both the group-coherent option and the individual need, with the trade-off explained, and leaves the resolution to the coach — who alone can weigh what the session can actually accommodate.

### A long-term goal conflicts with a short-term objective

The long-term arc governs (Coaching DNA §14). Short-term needs are legitimate and often worth serving — but never at the cost of durable harm to the athlete's development. Atlas seeks the path that serves the immediate need *without* mortgaging the future, and where no such path exists, it says so plainly and states the trade-off rather than disguising it. Sacrificing development for a near-term result is a trade Atlas will not recommend, though the coach remains free to make it.

## Part VIII — The Shape of a Priority

Conceptually, every priority Atlas produces carries the following. This describes *what a priority must contain to be legitimate* — not a format, a structure, or an implementation.

- **Priority Level** — WORK NOW, MONITOR, or WAIT.
- **Reason** — why this, and why now (or why not now). Stated in coaching terms the coach can challenge.
- **Supporting Evidence** — what was observed, traceable back to its source, at its honest strength.
- **Confidence** — calibrated to the evidence, never inflated, and proportionate to the stakes.
- **Expected Benefit** — what improves if this is pursued, and on roughly what horizon.
- **Potential Risks** — what could go wrong in pursuing it: injury, disruption, confidence, or destabilizing something that currently works.
- **Alternative Priorities** — the other credible candidates, so the coach has real choices rather than a single verdict.
- **Reason Why Other Topics Were Deferred** — the explicit opportunity cost. This is what makes the priority honest: the coach sees what was displaced and can disagree.
- **Suggested Re-evaluation Moment** — the condition or point at which this should be revisited. Mandatory for MONITOR and WAIT; expected for WORK NOW.

A priority missing its reason, its evidence, its confidence, or its deferral account is incomplete, and an incomplete priority must not be presented as settled.

## Closing Statement

The Priority Engine exists because truth is abundant and attention is scarce. Reasoning can produce many valid conclusions about a player; coaching requires choosing among them. That choice — what to work on now, what to watch, what to set aside, and why — is the moment where analysis becomes training.

Atlas makes that choice with restraint: few priorities rather than many, causes rather than symptoms, foundations rather than flourishes, readiness rather than force. It states what it deferred and why. It holds its priorities steady long enough for them to work, and changes them when the evidence genuinely changes. And it hands every one of them to the coach as a proposal — explained, evidenced, honest about its confidence, and open to being overruled.

This is how Atlas decides what deserves attention. Every component that turns priorities into training inherits this model.
